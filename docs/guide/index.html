1. Ending your turn in enemy range

Whoever hits first wins an even fight. So don't end your turn in range of an equal unit unless your tile is good enough. The tables say what's good enough.

How to read them: you stand on the row's tile. The enemy hits you first from the column's tile. Then you trade until one dies. Cell = your result, and both units' HP after the first hit.

Md tank can end in tank range anywhere. A tank never wins in md range, own city included. A class gap is worth more than the first hit.
Mechs only end in tank range on their own city. Tanks can end in mech range on plains or better, unless the mech is on a mountain or its own city.
Anti-air can end in infantry and recon range anywhere, and in mech range on plains or better. Infantry attacking a full anti-air from a road dies to the counter.
Infantry only wins against infantry (on a city) and mechs (on your own city).

3. Maps

  • Woods between two armies are open ground for even fights.
  • Pushes happen where a 3-star tile inside their range faces 2-star-or-worse tiles. Count those per side to check a map's balance.
  • Roads into the enemy hurt whoever uses them to approach.
  • A front city you own is a fortress for equal units. Taking it from them removes theirs.

4. Standoffs

If nobody can end their turn in range safely, nobody should, and you can always pass. Solved: infantry vs infantry and tank vs tank on empty 4x4 and 5x5 are draws. On 3x3 (in range on turn one) the first player wins. With a city or HQ to take, the first player wins.

Units that outrange the enemy's threat break it: rockets fire at 5, infantry threaten 4.

5. Tempo and value

Moving first is worth ~3000 funds (12x12 symmetric maps, one base each). Extra starting funds for the second player, decisive games first-second: +0: 20-8, +2000: 18-10, +3000: 31-25, +4000: 10-18.

Unit worth, as share of cost, tuned by self-play: inf 198%, tank 139%, aa 107%, mech 104%, neo 96%, art 90%, recon 85%, rockets 81%, md 73%. Small maps, fast games, this engine's play. An md tank beats a tank, but two tanks cost less.

With a day limit, army doesn't count. Turn it into properties or the HQ.

6. Not known yet

  • Whole-game strategy: build orders, when to push. The engine isn't strong enough yet; next step is mining its games for what winners do differently.
  • Fog: infantry see 2 and threaten 4, so you could end in range unseen. Probably breaks standoffs. To be solved on small boards.
  • Artillery and rockets in exact solves.
  • No day limit (HQ and rout only).
  • Fights of two or three units a side.

7. Problems with no-fog AW

Measured where marked, else opinion.

  1. Waiting is rewarded (measured on small boards).
  2. Moving first is worth ~3000 funds (measured).
  3. Woods don't matter in even fights (measured).
  4. Day-limit games are decided by counting properties.
  5. With no luck, a lot of fights are decided before they start.
